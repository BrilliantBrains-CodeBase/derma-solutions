## What is Vitiligo?

Understanding the skin condition and its impact on your life.

Play

Vitiligo is a skin condition characterized by the loss of pigmentation, resulting in white patches on the skin.

It occurs when the melanocytes, the cells responsible for producing pigment, stop functioning or die.

While the exact cause is unknown, it is believed to be an autoimmune disorder and can affect individuals of any age, gender, or skin type.

**Key Features of Vitiligo:**

- Affects any area of the body, including the face, hands, and extremities.
- Can progress over time if left untreated.
- Not contagious but may have a genetic component.

## What Causes Vitiligo?

Identifying the triggers behind this skin condition.

![Autoimmune](https://dermasolutions.co.in/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/noun-autoimmune-6988778-326F7D.svg)

#### Autoimmune Response

The immune system attacks the melanocytes, leading to pigment loss.

![Genetics](https://dermasolutions.co.in/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/noun-genetics-7322142-326F7D.svg)

#### Genetics

A family history of vitiligo increases the risk.

![Environmental](https://dermasolutions.co.in/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/noun-environmental-6025121-326F7D.svg)

#### Environmental Factors

Sunburn, chemical exposure, or stress can act as triggers.

![Antioxidants](https://dermasolutions.co.in/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/noun-antioxidants-2033098-326F7D.svg)

#### Oxidative Stress

Imbalance of free radicals and antioxidants in the skin.

**Types of Vitiligo:**

• Segmental Vitiligo: Localized patches on one side of the body.

• Non-Segmental Vitiligo: Symmetrical patches on both sides of the body.

• Focal Vitiligo: Limited to a few small areas.

• Mucosal Vitiligo: Affects areas like lips and inside the mouth.

## What Are the Symptoms of Vitiligo?

Recognizing the signs for early intervention.

![Indian Woman Vitiligo Patches On Hand](https://dermasolutions.co.in/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/Indian-Woman-Vitiligo-patches-on-Hand.jpg)

**Common Symptoms:**

- White patches on the skin, especially in sun-exposed areas.
- Premature whitening or graying of hair on the scalp, beard, or eyebrows.
- Loss of pigment in the mucous membranes, such as the mouth or nose.

**Diagnosis:**

Our dermatologists use advanced diagnostic tools, including:

- **Physical Examination:** To evaluate the extent and pattern of depigmentation.
- **Wood’s Lamp Test:** A specialized UV light to detect pigmentation loss.
- **Blood Tests:** To check for autoimmune conditions or nutrient deficiencies.
